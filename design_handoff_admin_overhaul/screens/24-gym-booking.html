Spec The renter booking page

The page a gym renter opens from the link in their confirmation email. The only screen in this set that is not an admin screen — the person using it has no login, no session and no reason to know the admin exists. Fix list: Task 17 (domain and shell), Task 18 (this pattern).

A request is a basket, not a booking

The thing being built is a set of hour slots across many dates — Tuesday 6–7 PM, Wednesday 5–6 PM and 7–8 PM. Hours on a day need not touch, dates need not be adjacent, and the selection must survive moving to another month.

There is no contiguity rule. Adjacent hours on one date simply print as a range in the summary — 5–7 PM — while a gap prints as two entries, 5–6 PM, 7–8 PM.

Where it lives

  • timothystl.org/gym/book/<token> — not admin.timothystl.org. This URL is pasted into emails to people outside the church. Serving it from the admin host advertises the admin origin to every renter and stops you requiring a session on admin.* at the edge.
  • Permanent redirect from the old address; renters have confirmation emails going back months.
  • The Private booking link field on /gym-rentals/groups/edit/<id> is where the wrong address comes from. Fix it there too (Task 19) or every group re-copies the old one.

The shell

  • Public-site green masthead #4A5E3A with the logo and tagline, then the 3px gold rule #C9973A — the header every page on timothystl.org wears. No admin sidebar, no context bar, no ⌘K.
  • Beneath it a navy band #1E2D4A: Gym Rental in Lora 26px, the group name in gold, and the rate and hours as one line at 13px #AFC0D2. That was three stacked boxes; it is one block.
  • Body on sand, cards #FFFDF9 / 1px #E7DFD1 / radius 12 with the soft two-layer shadow. Foundations values throughout, so the page matches timothystl.org/worship rather than matching nothing.

Step 1 · Choose your slots

  • The month grid is a multi-date picker. Tapping a day opens its hours beneath the grid; the hours you have already picked on other days stay picked.
  • Four day states, and none of them is carried by colour alone — which is what the red and green dots were doing. Open: navy numeral in an outlined cell. In your request: filled navy cell with the hour count beneath. Fully booked and not rentable: grey numeral, no cell, not clickable, told apart by their tooltip. The legend disappears with the dots; there is nothing left to decode.
  • The day panel opens in the mist tint so it reads as attached to the grid above it, and says in plain words that hours need not be adjacent.
  • Recurring dates is the second tab, not a competing flow — it fills the same request. Range, weekdays, hours, then Add these to my request: it adds to the basket and reports how many it added, and never replaces what is already there. A season of Tuesday practices is four taps.

Step 2 · Your request

  • The part that does not exist today, and the reason the current page is hard to trust. One row per date in date order: date, the times, hours, cost, and a ✕ to drop the date. Individual times come off by tapping them again in the grid.
  • Totals at the foot, with Clear all as a quiet red text button — never a filled button next to the primary action.
  • Empty state says what to do, not that something is missing: “Nothing picked yet. Choose a date above, then the hours you need.”
  • On a phone this panel pins to the foot of the viewport. A request built across five dates cannot be checked from memory.

Step 3 · Confirm

  • Name, email, phone, and what the space is for. Field column caps at 640px (Task 19), not the full width of the window.
  • The insurance notice lives here, above the button, where it is actionable — not at the top of the page ahead of the calendar. It reads “before your first rental date”, since a request can span months.
  • Request this booking — a request, because the office prices and approves it. One line beneath says so: nothing is charged now.
  • After submit, the same page is replaced by a plain confirmation: what was requested, what happens next, the insurance reminder again. Not a redirect to a bare thanks page.

On a phone

  • Design at 390px first — this page is opened from an email, on a phone, standing in a hallway.
  • The month grid stays seven columns. A list of dates loses the shape of the week, which is how people think about a gym. Cells go to 44px minimum.
  • Hour chips become a two-column grid. One column wastes the width; three makes them too small to hit.
  • Your request is a pinned foot bar showing the rows and the total, expanding on tap.

What not to change

The month grid with per-day availability is right, and it is why this screen cannot become the list-and-drawer pattern. Same reasoning as Blocked dates: you book a gym by looking at a month. Keep the calendar; fix what it is saying.
