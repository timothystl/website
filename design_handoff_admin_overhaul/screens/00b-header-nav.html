The shell — sidebar and context bar

The sidebar stays. It is the navigation, exactly as Foundations specifies it — twenty-one sections in five groups is more than a horizontal bar can hold honestly, and a list you can read top to bottom beats chips you have to scroll. What the build got wrong was not the sidebar; it was hiding it behind a hamburger.

Above the content sits a slim context bar — the one good idea from the editor's top bar, kept and cut down. It answers "where am I" at a glance and carries the two global actions. It does not navigate.

The sidebar

  • Built exactly as Foundations → The sidebar specifies: 228px, #1D3557, its own scroll, group labels in gold, 7px dot on top-level rows and an elbow on the five children of Pages, active row raised to #27496E with inset 0 0 0 1px rgba(255,255,255,.16) and an #E0A82E dot, gold count badges, ⌘K line and Sign out in the foot.
  • Always on screen. No transform:translateX(-100%), no .is-open, no backdrop, no hamburger. Content sits beside it, not under it.
  • The only responsive rule: below 900px it may become a slide-over with a hamburger — a phone genuinely cannot spare 228px. On every laptop and desktop it is fixed and visible.
  • Permissions hide whole groups, so a ministry leader's sidebar is short rather than mostly disabled.

The context bar

  • 46px, #1E2D4A, padding 0 26px, sitting above the content column only — it starts where the sidebar ends and does not run behind it. The two navies read as one shell.
  • Left: the trail, as two parts. Group name at 500 12.5px #8598B0, a / separator at #4E6180, then the section at 600 13.5px #FFFFFF. Dashboard's group reads “Admin”.
  • If the current section has a waiting count, an amber pill follows it — “3 waiting”. Same tones as the sidebar badge.
  • Inside an editor, a third crumb is appended: Website / Pages / Plan a Visit, with the page's address beneath at 11.5px #6B7F99. That is the only place the trail goes three deep.
  • Right: the ⌘K chip and “View site”, both white-space:nowrap. Nothing else — no “Connect”, no outbound arrow glyphs. Username and Sign out stay in the sidebar foot where they already are.
  • It is not navigation. The crumbs are not links, there are no tabs, and nothing in it opens a menu. Its whole job is to say where you are, and to put the two things you reach for from any screen within one click.
  • Back-links that used to live in the white util bar (“← Dashboard” on the gym sub-screens) go in the content area, above the h1, at 600 13px #2E7EA6 — not in the context bar.

What changes in the build

  • Delete transform:translateX(-100%), .is-open, .sidebar-backdrop, .sidebar-toggle and the toggle script from admin/helpers.js. Keep sidebarShell() and all its permission and badge logic — it was right.
  • Give the content column margin-left:228px (or make the shell a two-column flex, which is cleaner) and drop .wrap{max-width:860px} — that narrow column was a symptom of the sidebar being absent, not a layout choice.
  • Replace the white .util-bar with the context bar. Its extraLinks argument keeps working; the links just render in the content area instead.
  • “Connect” is removed, not relocated — it linked to an unrelated app and does not belong in the admin chrome. Sign out returns to the sidebar foot, in gold, as specified.
  • Both editors keep their own 58px navy bar with undo/redo, device switcher, draft state, Preview and Publish. In an editor that bar replaces the context bar — an editor is full-screen and keeps the sidebar collapsed to its pages rail. It is the one screen that does not wear this shell.
Get this wrong and it shows
  • Putting section tabs, group chips, or a menu in the context bar. The sidebar navigates; the bar reports.
  • A hamburger on a desktop.
  • Making the context bar a lighter colour than the sidebar — two different navies in one shell reads as two products.
  • Duplicating Sign out in both the bar and the sidebar foot.
